Saudi Arabia's digital economy is growing fast, and customers increasingly expect to find, compare and contact businesses online before they visit or buy. For a small business in Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam or anywhere in the Kingdom, the basics below make the biggest difference.

## 1. An Arabic-first website that works on phones

Most Saudi customers browse on their phones and search in Arabic. Your website should:

- Be designed in Arabic first, with proper right-to-left layouts, not just translated.
- Load quickly on mobile data and make calling or messaging you a single tap.
- Have separate Arabic and English pages if you serve both audiences, so search engines can show the right version.
- Show your opening hours, location and how to order or book clearly.

## 2. Payments customers already use

If you sell online, offer the payment methods your customers trust. In Saudi Arabia that usually means mada cards alongside Visa, Mastercard and Apple Pay. Work with a licensed payment provider that supports these methods and settles to a local bank account.

## 3. E-invoicing (FATOORAH)

The Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA) requires VAT-registered businesses to issue electronic invoices, and is connecting businesses to its systems in stages. If you are VAT registered:

- Check whether your invoicing or accounting software is compatible with ZATCA's requirements.
- Find out when your business is due to connect, based on ZATCA's announcements.
- Keep your product, customer and VAT details clean, because e-invoicing makes errors more visible.

Requirements change over time, so always confirm the details on ZATCA's official website or with your accountant.

## 4. Look after customer data

Saudi Arabia's Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL) sets rules for how businesses collect, use and store personal data. In practice, small businesses should:

- Collect only the information they need.
- Explain clearly, in a privacy notice, how they use it.
- Keep it secure, with strong passwords, two-factor login and regular backups.
- Check where their software providers store data.

## 5. Be easy to find locally

- Create and verify your Google Business Profile, with accurate hours and photos.
- Make sure your business name, phone number and location match everywhere online.
- Ask happy customers for reviews and reply to every one.

## 6. Automate the repetitive work

Once the basics are in place, automation saves real time: answering common WhatsApp questions, sending booking reminders, chasing unpaid invoices and preparing weekly reports. Start with one task that takes your team hours every week, prove it works, then add the next.

## Where to start

You do not need everything at once. A fast Arabic-first website, a complete Google profile and a clear way to contact you will already put you ahead of many competitors.
