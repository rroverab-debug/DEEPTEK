Small businesses in Oman rely on technology every day, from email and accounting to point-of-sale systems and social media. When it works, nobody notices. When it fails, it can stop the business for hours or days. This checklist covers the basics every small business in Muscat, Sohar, Salalah or anywhere in Oman should have in place.

## 1. Professional email and shared files

- Use a business email address on your own domain, not a personal free account.
- Choose one platform for email, calendars and files, such as Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace.
- Keep shared files in one organised place instead of on individual laptops.

## 2. Accounts in the company's name

Make sure your domain, email platform, website hosting and social media accounts are registered to the business, with at least two trusted people who can access them. When a staff member or supplier leaves, you should not lose access.

## 3. Strong logins

- Turn on two-factor login for email, banking, accounting and social media.
- Use a password manager so everyone has strong, unique passwords.
- Remove access promptly when someone leaves.

## 4. Backups you have tested

- Back up important data automatically, every day.
- Keep at least one copy away from the office, for example in the cloud.
- Try restoring a file every few months to prove the backup works.

## 5. Protected devices

- Keep laptops and phones updated automatically.
- Use reputable security software on every computer.
- Encrypt laptops so a lost device does not mean lost data.

## 6. Staff awareness

Most attacks start with a convincing email or message. Teach your team to check the sender, avoid unexpected links and attachments, and confirm payment requests by phone. A short reminder every few months goes a long way.

## 7. Personal data protection

Oman's Personal Data Protection Law (Royal Decree 6/2022) sets rules for how businesses handle personal data. Collect only what you need, keep it secure, explain how you use it, and check where your software providers store data. Confirm the current requirements with an adviser if you handle sensitive information.

## 8. A plan for when things go wrong

Write down who to call and what to do if email stops working, a laptop is stolen or an account is hacked. Keep the list somewhere you can reach even when your systems are down.

## 9. Someone responsible for IT

Even a small business needs someone accountable for IT, whether that is a team member or an outside provider. Regular checks catch small problems before they become expensive ones.
