Ask five agencies in Dubai for a website quote and you can get five very different numbers. That is not always because someone is overcharging. A "website" can mean a one-page profile, a bilingual company site, an online store or a full customer portal, and each one takes a different amount of work.

This guide explains what actually sets the price, so you can compare quotes fairly and pay for what your business needs.

## The five things that set the price

### 1. How many pages and templates you need

A small company site might have a home page, a services page, an about page and a contact page. A restaurant may add a menu and a reservations page. Each new type of page needs its own design and build, so the number of templates matters more than the raw number of pages.

### 2. One language or two

In the UAE, many businesses need English and Arabic. A good Arabic version is not just translated text. It needs right-to-left layouts, an Arabic font that reads well, and its own page addresses so Google can show it to Arabic searchers. Done properly, it adds real work, and it is usually worth it.

### 3. Features beyond information

Online ordering, table booking, payments, customer accounts, appointment calendars and connections to your other systems are where costs rise fastest. Each feature needs to be designed, built, tested and secured.

### 4. Who writes the content and supplies the photos

Copywriting, translation and photography are often left out of quotes. If you need help with these, ask for them to be listed as separate items so you can see the full price.

### 5. What happens after launch

Hosting, security updates, backups, small changes and SEO all continue after the site goes live. Some agencies include a period of support. Others charge for every change. Ask before you sign.

## What to ask for in every quote

- A list of pages and features included, in writing.
- Whether the Arabic version is included and who translates it.
- Who owns the domain, the hosting account and the website files.
- What hosting, maintenance and support cost each month or year.
- How many rounds of changes are included.
- When you will see progress, and what you approve at each stage.

If a quote cannot answer these clearly, it is hard to compare it with anything else.

## How to avoid paying twice

The most expensive website is the one you rebuild a year later. It usually happens for one of these reasons:

- **It is slow on mobile.** Most visitors in the UAE browse on their phones. A slow site loses enquiries and ranks lower on Google.
- **It cannot be found.** Without page titles, descriptions and a basic SEO setup, a beautiful site can stay invisible.
- **You do not control it.** If the domain or hosting is registered in someone else's name, moving away can be painful. Make sure accounts are in your company's name.
- **It was built for today only.** If you plan to add online orders or a second branch, say so early, so the site can grow without starting again.

## A sensible way to start

You do not need every feature on day one. Many small businesses start with a fast, bilingual site that explains what they do and makes it easy to call, WhatsApp or book. Once it brings in enquiries, add online ordering, automation or a customer portal in stages.
