In the UAE, WhatsApp is often the first place a customer goes to ask a question. "Are you open now?" "Do you have a table for six at eight?" "Do you deliver to Al Barsha?" For a busy restaurant or cafe, answering every message by hand means slow replies at the exact moments the team is busiest.

WhatsApp automation answers the routine questions instantly and passes the rest to your staff. Here is how it works and how to do it properly.

## What WhatsApp automation can handle

- **Opening hours, location and parking.** The questions you answer twenty times a day.
- **Menu questions.** Sending the latest menu, highlighting dishes or answering "do you have vegan options?"
- **Table bookings.** Collecting the date, time and party size, checking availability and confirming.
- **Order status.** Telling customers whether their order is being prepared or on its way, when it is connected to your ordering system.
- **Catering and event enquiries.** Collecting the details your manager needs before calling back.
- **Reviews and feedback.** Asking happy guests to leave a Google review after their visit, with their permission.

## What it should not do alone

Complaints, allergies, refunds and anything unusual should go to a person. A good setup recognises these messages and hands them to the right staff member with the conversation so far, so the customer does not repeat themselves.

## App or platform: which one do you need?

The free **WhatsApp Business app** works on one phone and is fine for replying by hand. For automation, several team members, and connections to your booking or ordering systems, you need the **WhatsApp Business Platform**. It is provided by Meta, directly or through approved providers.

The platform has rules worth knowing:

- Customers must agree to receive messages from you before you send them offers.
- Messages you start outside an active conversation use pre-approved templates.
- There may be charges per conversation, depending on the type of message and the country.

Check the current pricing and policies on Meta's WhatsApp Business pages before you start, as they change from time to time.

## Where AI fits

A simple menu-based bot ("press 1 for bookings") works for some businesses. AI assistants go further: they understand questions written in normal English or Arabic, including mixed messages, and answer from your own information, such as your menu, hours and policies.

The key is to keep the AI grounded in your real information and to give it clear limits. It should never invent prices, promise a table that is not available or give allergy advice. When it is not sure, it should hand over to a person.

## Protect your customers' data

Customer names, phone numbers and order history are personal data. The UAE's Personal Data Protection Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021) sets rules for how businesses handle it. Collect only what you need, keep it secure, and use providers that explain clearly where and how they store data.

## How to get started

1. List the ten questions your team answers most on WhatsApp.
2. Write the correct answer for each, in English and Arabic.
3. Decide which messages must always go to a person.
4. Start with these basics, measure how many chats are fully answered, then add bookings or orders.
